# 0056: A tenant's searches over its share of the workers wait off the workers in a bounded queue, and are refused with 429 past its size

**Status:** Accepted after independent review (two rounds, changes applied), 2026-11-25

## Context

[ADR 0047](0047-search-slots.md) orders searches with a gate whose waiters and overflow runners both sit on a REST worker, and it never refuses. Measured on the emulator and RustFS ([the spec's evidence](../specs/search-slots/VERIFIED.md); the 100-client run is in the same file), with the noisy index cold behind a 4 MiB resident bound: with 100 noisy clients (below the 128 workers) a quiet tenant's p99 is 1.8 ms, gate on or off; with 200, every worker is held by a noisy search waiting on the store, a quiet search waits for a free worker for about 1 s (p99 1,849 ms off, 1,930 ms on), and the gate changes nothing. Eviction of the quiet tenant's pages and fetch concurrency are not the cause. The limiting resource is workers.

What exists today ([ADR 0006](0006-http-stack.md)): `workers` blocking threads (128) take connections from one FIFO of accepted connections, at most `max_connections` (1,024) open; the next is answered 503 and closed. That FIFO is already a node-wide queue, as stock OpenSearch's `thread_pool.search.queue_size` (1,000, a fixed pool of 1.5 x cores + 1 threads, `es_rejected_execution_exception` with 429 when full) is, and it has the same flaw: it is first come, first served, so a tenant with many requests in flight fills every worker and the queue ahead of a quiet tenant. The cap is not a setting of the node (`ServerConfig::max_connections` is not read from the environment).

## Decision

**The hook.** `oss-rest` stays tenant-blind. A route may carry an *admission hook* (a trait object installed by the node, `Fn(&Request) -> Admission`), called in `conn.rs` after the guard, authorizer, content-type and query-parameter checks and before a body buffer is taken or `100 Continue` is sent. The hook gets only the request's method, path and parameters (not the stream, not a body), and returns `Run(guard)` (the guard is dropped when the response is sent, on every path, as ADR 0047's), `Defer(tenant)` or `Refuse`. `tenant` is an opaque key; the node's hook derives it as ADR 0047 does, from the first plain index name in the path (its bucket group, else the index), and a path with none, or a route with no hook, runs as today. Searches by alias, wildcard, `/_search` with no index or `_msearch` with indices in the body are not tenant-keyed here, so they run unqueued: a limitation, as the reserve guarding searches only is. Resolving them needs cluster state, which stays out of REST; the node's hook could take it later. Reader and writer crates do not gain a dependency: the hook is built in `oss-node`.

**Parking is replay.** On `Defer`, the server copies the head bytes and any bytes read past it (`unread` input, a pipelined request or the start of a body) into one entry, takes the stream out of the worker's loop and returns the worker to its channel. Nothing else of the request survives: no `Request`, no borrowed buffer, no body buffer, no `Timed`. A resumed entry is served as a new connection whose input is the entry's bytes followed by the socket: it is parsed again from the head, runs every check again (they have no side effect before the body is read, so running them twice is correct), calls the hook again, and gets a fresh `Timed` and clock. The worker's channel item becomes an enum, a new connection or a resumed one.

**Admission** (one predicate, `eligible(tenant)`, used by both the hook and the offer scan so they cannot drift; the hook's decision, one short critical section on one mutex that never wraps a search): the search runs at once when the tenant runs fewer than its share, `max(1, floor(workers * OSS_SEARCH_TENANT_SHARE_PCT / 100))`, searches, **or** the node runs fewer than `workers - OSS_SEARCH_RESERVED_WORKERS` searches. The second clause is what the reserve is: a tenant over its share can take at most `workers - reserved` workers, however early it arrives, so a tenant under its share always finds `reserved` workers left for searches. Otherwise the search is deferred, or refused when the queue already holds `OSS_SEARCH_QUEUE_SIZE` entries. The reserve guards searches only: bulk and other routes take any free worker (a limitation, as 0047's).

**The queue.** Per-tenant FIFO deques, plus one ring of tenants that have entries, so one tenant's backlog cannot hide another tenant's eligible entry. Offering scans the ring once from a rotating start for the first tenant for which `eligible` holds (under its share, or the node under `workers - reserved`), takes the head of its deque, and **reserves** the slot (the tenant's running count is incremented under the same lock, so a fresh arrival sees it); the cost is bounded by the number of tenants queued, at most `OSS_SEARCH_QUEUE_SIZE`, in microseconds. Resumed entries go to a separate deque the workers check before the channel, so they never need channel capacity and a full channel cannot lose one. An entry keeps its enqueue time, and so its deadline, for its whole life. The reservation is an RAII token created at offer time and carried on the channel item; the replayed connection's hook consumes it instead of asking `eligible` again, so a replay never defers a second time and never double counts, and the token returns the tenant's running count on every exit path, including a replay that is denied, 404, 400 or closed before the hook runs.

**Expiry, dead peers and shutdown.** There is no new thread. An idle worker blocks in the channel with `recv_timeout(idle_poll)` (50 ms), not `recv`, so it wakes to sweep and to take a resumed entry; offering a resumed entry also sends a wake token through the channel (an enum item) and counts the entry in `queued`, so a worker serving an idle keep-alive connection yields for it. One sweeper runs at a time (`try_lock` and a last-sweep time, at most one sweep per `idle_poll`); the worker that ends a search triggers a sweep by the same rule and does not wait for it. A sweep: an entry past `OSS_SEARCH_QUEUE_WAIT_MS` (default 30,000) is answered 429 and closed; an entry whose peer has closed (a non-blocking `peek` returns end of stream; a peer that sent bytes and then closed is found on resume, not before) is dropped and its counts returned. When every worker is busy and none ends a search for a while, nothing sweeps, and the deadline is then met when a search ends or a worker idles: the bound is "not before the deadline, and by the next search end or idle poll", stated as that. A dead peer costs a queue slot, a share count and a descriptor until the next sweep, not until the deadline. A 429 or 503 from a sweeper is the small fixed answer, written under the write timeout the acceptor uses for its 503, so a stuck peer cannot hold a sweeping worker. `Server::shutdown` answers every entry 503 and closes it. A resumed entry's connection restarts with the request-timeout rule of a new connection.

**Refusal.** 429 with the OpenSearch error body `es_rejected_execution_exception` (type, and the message shape the Java shell's recorded fixture holds, checked rather than assumed), and a `Retry-After` header, which OpenSearch does not send: a deliberate extra. The connection closes, because an unread body is still in the socket and the server already closes after a refusal with an unread body. The refusal is a node rule (a tenant's share and the queue's size), where OpenSearch refuses only when its pool's queue is full: recorded as a difference in `docs/compatibility.md` when built.

**Memory and descriptors.** An entry is at most `2 * head_buffer_size` of copied bytes (the head and the read-ahead; `head_buffer_size` is `max_initial_line + max_header_bytes + 4`, 20 KiB by default), allocated when it is queued (`// ALLOC:` with that bound), plus the stream. At start the node reserves `OSS_SEARCH_QUEUE_SIZE * 2 * head_buffer_size` from `request_bodies` and holds it for its life, so a full queue cannot starve body buffers, and a node whose pool cannot hold it does not start (a queue of 1,000 is about 40 MiB; the queue is off by default, so this bites only when enabled). Descriptors are bounded by `max_connections`, which entries count toward once, as open connections: the acceptor's rule is unchanged. Parked entries do not count in `queued`, the figure that makes an idle keep-alive connection yield.

**Settings** (node scope, `OSS_*`): `OSS_SEARCH_QUEUE_SIZE` (default 0: no hook is installed and a search takes no step it does not take today), `OSS_SEARCH_TENANT_SHARE_PCT` (25, 1 to 100), `OSS_SEARCH_RESERVED_WORKERS` (8, below `workers`), `OSS_SEARCH_QUEUE_WAIT_MS` (30,000, up to 600,000). A start refuses a queue size above `max_connections - workers`. `OSS_HTTP_MAX_CONNECTIONS` (default 1,024, the present value, now readable) is its own change in the same milestone, committed apart. ADR 0047's gate and this queue are alternatives: a start with both on is refused, because a gate wait would hold the worker the queue exists to free. The usage route reports `search_queue`: running, deferred, deferred ever, resumed, refused 429, expired, dropped for a closed peer, the longest wait. Fixed-size counters, nothing per tenant.

## Consequences

- Expected, not measured: a tenant over its share holds at most `workers - reserved` workers, and a quiet tenant's search finds one; below saturation, or with one tenant alone, nothing waits. The measurement that decides the default is the cold-cache test at 100 and 200 noisy clients, queue on against off, on the emulator and RustFS: quiet p99, search p99 below saturation within 5%, no store request added (none are), and a soak with peers closing and searches failing while parked showing every count returned.
- A search can be refused by a rule of this node, with 429, as OpenSearch refuses when its queue is full; clients written for OpenSearch retry it. This amends ADR 0047 for the lane's purpose: its status line is updated when this is accepted, and its text stays correct for the gate it describes.
- The spec's acceptance criteria include the `docs/compatibility.md` row (a 429 from a node rule, `Retry-After`, the connection closed) and a test that the guard and authorizer have no side effect before the body, since a replay runs them twice.
- The REST server gains a hook, an enum channel item and a parked state; its tests pin that a parked entry holds no body buffer, that `Expect: 100-continue` is answered only on resume, that pipelined bytes survive, that an offer reaches a worker blocked idle in the channel within one poll, that the offer scan and admission agree when a tenant over its share finds the node under `workers - reserved`, that a closed peer and an expired entry return their counts, and that shutdown closes entries.
- It stays off by default until measured, per the switch rule.
- Not here: a per-tenant limit on store reads in flight and a per-tenant share of the resident page budget. At 100 noisy clients the quiet tenant is unaffected while the noisy tenant evicts the shared 4 MiB bound and reads about 17,800 pages from the store (VERIFIED.md), so neither is the cause of the 200-client slowdown.
