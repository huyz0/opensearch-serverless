# 0025: Node pack collection

**Status:** Proposed. An independent review (2026-11-21) found that the first draft does not clear; this version answers its findings, and the five decisions at the end are the owner's. Until it is Accepted and built, `serverless.segments.node_pack.enabled` ([ADR 0024](0024-node-packs-in-the-writer.md)) cannot be turned on anywhere.

## Context

A pack is deleted only when nothing names it. The Java's `NodePackCollector` does this with in-memory state: it tracks each pack from when it first saw it, judges it 15 minutes later, deletes it after two unreferenced sightings 60 seconds apart, and rechecks a pack found referenced on a backoff capped at 6 hours. Four properties of that design break the owner's rules ([principles](../principles.md#cost-s3-requests-are-the-bill)) or do not carry over to nodes that own nothing:

1. **A capped recheck costs `n` manifest GETs every 6 hours per pack for as long as the pack lives.** With no cap it is `O(log lifetime)` per pack per shard (about 19 a year: provisional arithmetic), at the price of garbage found at most twice as late as it died, and per-pack space amplification that is not bounded: one idle shard's small blob pins a pack of up to 64 MiB holding other shards' dead blocks.
2. **A long-lived pack at ordinal `k` stops the ledger's `collectedBelow` prefix.** After any restart, or in a reaper pass, the epoch is re-read from `collectedBelow` to `high`: one header GET per ordinal, including the 404s of deleted slots, about 86,000 a day at one pack a second. That is the elapsed-time cost, and the Java's recheck cap hides it.
3. **The state is in memory.** A restart, or a reaper that runs once, starts from nothing: it cannot judge a pack until 15 minutes after it first saw it, and it forgets the schedule. The ledger holds only `(epoch, collectedBelow, high)` per epoch and the Java reads exactly that (version 1).
4. **Referenced-ness is judged from the pack's header,** by the manifests of the shards named in it, plus views and snapshots. A shard not named in the header that nonetheless names the pack by location (a clone, a restore, a split) is invisible to it.

## Decision

- **Rotate the epoch every 1,024 ordinals.** A new epoch is one ledger swap per 1,024 packs. A pinned pack then pins one bounded epoch, not the node's lifetime: the re-read from `collectedBelow` costs at most 1,024 headers per epoch, once, and the in-memory empty-slot set is bounded by it. Wire-compatible: the Java's ledger already holds many epochs. The ledger read cap (256 KiB, about 10,000 epochs) is addressed by dropping an epoch from the ledger once every one of its ordinals is collected; the newest epoch is never dropped.
- **Reserve the high ahead.** Before spending ordinals `base..base+7` the writer raises the recorded `high` to `base+8` by a monotone-max compare-and-swap (the Java's `advance` takes the max), and a failed record blocks packing (fails closed). No pack ever lands at or above the recorded `high`, so a reader of a Rust ledger needs no probe past it (the Java's 16-slot probe still covers Java-written epochs, and a run of 16 spent ordinals in a dead Java node's epoch still leaks the packs behind it: stated, not fixed). This is one extra ledger swap per eight packs, on the leader's critical path: about 12% more PUTs on the pack path, to be counted in the R11 measurement, not assumed free.
- **Reference sources are one abstraction the collector requires.** `PackReferences` answers "does anything name this pack" over: commit manifests (a bucketed member's from its bucket manifest, through the descriptor), frozen views and points in time, snapshots, and clones, restores and splits that share blobs. Like `Pinned` in the janitor ([`crates/janitor/src/gc/pins.rs`](../../crates/janitor/src/gc/pins.rs)) it has no "none" implementation. A check enforces the rule that a new place that records a pack location must register: every constructor of a node-packed `FileLocation` outside the writer is in an allow-list file, with a planted violation in `scripts/selftest-checks.py` and a row in [mechanical-gates](../mechanical-gates.md). A cluster whose snapshots cannot be read (a Java node made them) is never judged: pack collection stops there, which is correct and is said in the log.
- **Views are enumerated by a LIST, once per pass, and passes are floored.** The points-in-time inventory is a LIST ([`crates/meta/src/pit/sweep.rs`](../../crates/meta/src/pit/sweep.rs)). That is priced like a PUT, so a pass runs at most every 10 minutes and judges every due pack in it, sharing the manifest reads; a pass with nothing due makes no request and no LIST. A view still being captured halts judgement; a capture placeholder older than its keep-alive is treated as dead, so one crashed freeze does not stop collection cluster-wide.
- **Clocks.** The swap deadline is checked immediately before the register swap, on `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` (which keeps counting across a suspend), with the swap call's own time limit and retries below the margin; a pack is judged at three deadlines (15 minutes). No lease is claimed to fence a paused writer: the shard register's compare-and-swap fences only once a successor has moved it, so correctness rests on a stated bound, a pause shorter than the margin between the check and the swap, as every timeout in the system does. The collector's ages use the monotonic clock, so a forward step of the wall clock cannot make a young pack judgeable.
- **Dead nodes' packs.** Any live node may collect them: the ledger and packs are readable by anyone and updated by compare-and-swap. The trigger cannot be the lease naming log epochs ([ADR 0021](0021-proactive-takeover-from-the-node-log.md)): a lease is pruned after ten lifetimes expired, and a scaled-in node's lease names none. See decision 1 below.
- **Memory.** A tracked entry holds the pack's shard keys, not its header (a header may be 4 MiB), capped per entry and in total; tracked bytes are reserved from the node's pool (ADR 0004); at the cap the collector stops adding and says so. Headers are re-read at a recheck only when needed.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Trigger and age for a dead node's packs.** Recommended: a node's ledger is collected by the reapers of any node id the membership has ever listed, for as long as its ledger has an epoch not fully collected, and the age of a pack is its object's `LastModified` from the one HEAD a judgement needs (the cost is one HEAD per due pack, paid once; otherwise a pack the reaper did not watch land has no age and is never judged).
2. **The exception to "never elapsed time per index".** The recheck is logarithmic in a pack's life, which is not constant. Recommended: accept it as a recorded exception.
3. **Pack space amplification.** One long-lived blob pins its pack. Recommended: accept for now, and measure; a later merge or repack is its own decision.
4. **The 12% PUT cost of reserving the high ahead,** against a leaked pack per gap. Recommended: accept, and include it in the R11 measurement.
5. **Whether to build this at all before the R11 measurement** shows what packs save. Recommended: measure first with packs on and no collector in a bounded run (packs are written, never deleted), then decide.

## Consequences

- Nothing here is built; ADR 0024's switch stays off.
- Rules out: a capped recheck, a LIST of any pack prefix, collection by the owner only, judging while a reference source cannot be read, and any claim that a lease fences a paused publisher.
