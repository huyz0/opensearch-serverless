# Index State Management, native: policies, managed indices, explain

## Serves

R15.4's ISM, under [ADR 0077](../../adr/0077-index-state-management-is-ported-natively.md), which supersedes [ADR 0046](../../adr/0046-plugin-requests-are-proxied-to-java-nodes.md) for ISM. The owner's decision (2026-11-28): the stock plugins are ported to Rust, not proxied. A client of the stock Index Management plugin's ISM routes (`opensearch-project/index-management`) must work unchanged against a Rust node.

## Change

Five slices, one PR each, each behind the setting below. Nothing in lucene-rust applies: a search of that repository for index state management, managed indices, rollover and retention finds only Lucene merge policies and a docs example of an ISM policy. The building blocks are here: [rollover](../rollover-route/SPEC.md), [alias mutations](../alias-mutations/SPEC.md), [index blocks](../index-blocks/SPEC.md), snapshots, index delete.

1. **Policy store and routes.** `oss-meta::ism`: policies as a document set (a marker register naming immutable content blobs, the stored-scripts shape) under `ism/<tenant>/policies/`. Routes on `/_plugins/_ism` and `/_opendistro/_ism`: `PUT|GET|DELETE /policies/{id}` (PUT with `if_seq_no` and `if_primary_term` mapped onto the marker's version), `GET /policies`. A policy is validated at put: `default_state` exists, state names unique, every transition names a state, conditions are among `min_index_age`, `min_rollover_age`, `cron` (`min_doc_count` and size conditions are 400 at put: a count is a request per index over elapsed time, which the rules forbid; the owner may decide otherwise), and every action is one of the supported list below; one outside it is 400 naming it at put, never at run. Bounds: policy 1 MiB, 1,000 policies a tenant, 50 states, 20 actions a state.
2. **Managed indices and explain**, built in two PRs: 2a is `add`, `remove` and `explain` over the managed record alone (a literal index name; a wildcard or list is refused 400); 2b is `change_policy` and `retry` (each records its request on the managed index for the runner to apply, so they cost the policy read, the record's read and one replace: 5 GETs and 2 PUTs, and 3 GETs and 2 PUTs); 2c is `ism_template`, which needs its own design because matching an index name against every policy's templates must not read every policy: templates are kept in one aggregate document of the tenant, written under the policy put and delete and read once by an index create. The run register and the registry shard are created by `add` once the runner (slice 3) reads them, and the counts of AC-4 and AC-4b below grow by their requests then; until then `add` costs the policy read (2 GETs), the index record (1 GET) and the record's put (1 GET, 2 PUTs). The slice: A managed-index record (policy id and seq, current state, action, step, `step_seq`, retry count, `info`, `enabled_time`, last transition and rollover time, and `index_created`, the creation date copied from the descriptor `add` already reads) as a document set under `ism/<tenant>/managed/`. The marker inlines per index its name, policy id, state and `next_eval` (about 120 bytes, 120 KB at 1,000, checked by a size test), so one GET answers the runner for every index of a tenant and no per-index read happens until one is due, at most 1,000 managed indices a tenant (429 past). Routes: `POST /add/{index}` (with `policy_id`, `state`), `POST /remove/{index}`, `POST /change_policy/{index}`, `POST /retry/{index}`, `GET|POST /explain/{index}` and `/explain`. A name is a literal index; a wildcard expansion against the tenant's own index records is a later change, never a LIST. `ism_template` (`index_patterns`, `priority`) attaches a policy when an index is created: with ISM enabled the create reads the tenant's template aggregate once (2 GETs, or 1 when no policy has a template), at most one document however many policies there are, a bill per index created, not per time; an attach adds the managed record's write and the registry shard's write (the protocol below). Answers carry `updated_indices`, `failures`, `failed_indices` as the plugin does.
3. **The runner: transitions.** No poll per index. A managed index's next evaluation time is derived: the earliest of its state's transition conditions (`min_index_age` from `index_created` in the record, `min_rollover_age` from the recorded rollover time, `cron`) and the retry backoff of a failed step, stored as `next_eval` in the marker. A node sleeps until the earliest; nothing wakes on a timer that has nothing due, and `job_interval` is not used. Discovery is the alerting runner's registry shape: 256 registry markers `ism/registry/<0..255>` naming tenants with at least one managed index, read every `serverless.ism.refresh_ttl` (default 300 s), and the managed marker of the tenants a node places (top 2 nodes by the placement hash). The registry protocol is alerting's, restated: an `add` writes the record and marker, then **always CASes its tenant's shard** (adding the entry or rewriting it unchanged so the etag moves) and is acknowledged only after that CAS; a `remove` or delete that leaves the tenant no managed index reads the shard (etag E), re-reads the tenant's managed marker, and removes the entry by CAS against E only if the marker shows none; a shard holds at most 4,096 tenants and a tenant past it gets 429 at `add`. This slice builds its own copy; a later change merges it with alerting's. A tenant with no managed index costs nothing beyond its share of the 256 markers.
4. **The runner: actions.** `rollover` (condition `min_index_age`; `min_doc_count` and size conditions refused 400 at put), `delete`, `read_only`, `read_write` (the `index.blocks.write` setting), `snapshot` (the native snapshot take into a registered repository), `alias` (add and remove through the alias protocol), `replica_count` (accepted only for 0), `notification` (a channel posted through the alerting channels once that slice exists; refused at put before). Refused 400 at put with a message saying so: `force_merge` (no native merge request exists), `index_priority` (the setting has no effect here), `close`, `open`, `shrink`, `allocation`, `rollup`, `transform`. **Idempotence of a re-run step:** `delete` treats a missing index as success; `read_only` and `read_write` set a value; `alias` add and remove are repeatable; `rollover` is fenced by the alias CAS and a re-run that finds the alias already moved is success; `snapshot` names its snapshot `ism-<index>-<state entry time>` and treats "exists" as success. Every action is bounded by `serverless.ism.action_timeout` (default 5 min, below `step_timeout`); one past it is aborted and its outcome is a failure, so an action never outlives its claim. Each action is a step list with the plugin's step names (`attempt_rollover`, `attempt_delete` and so on) so `explain` reads the same.
5. **Retries, failure and stats.** A failed step is retried with the plugin's `retry` (`count`, `backoff`, `delay`) by deriving the next evaluation time, not by a timer; past the count the record is `failed` with the reason, and `POST /retry/{index}` re-arms it. The cluster settings `plugins.index_state_management.enabled`, `.job_interval` and `.history.enabled` are read from the settings registry; history indices (`.opendistro-ism-managed-index-history-*`) are not created (no system index exists), and `.history.enabled` is false here (the plugin's default is true) and setting it true is refused 400 with a message saying so.

**Execution and fencing.** The claim object is one small register per managed index, `ism/runs/<tenant>/<index>` (not the tenant's shared marker, so 1,000 indices never serialise on one register), holding `step_seq`, `status`, `claimed_at` and the compact outcome (state, action, step, retry). A step runs as: read the register (etag E), CAS it to `step_seq + 1` with status `running` (the claim: 1 PUT, no lease), run the action, CAS the outcome against the claim's etag (1 PUT). The record and the marker's `next_eval` are then updated, only when the state, action, step, retry or `next_eval` changed (the document set's 2 PUTs, retried on a lost marker swap); the marker is a hint the runner reads, the run register decides. The marker swap is retried at most 8 times with jitter; past that the step stays complete, `next_eval` stays stale and the next `refresh_ttl` evaluation corrects it (counted, never a failed step). **Register lifecycle:** `add` and an `ism_template` attach create the register with `step_seq` 0 and status `idle` (1 PUT); `remove` and the runner's cleanup of a deleted index delete it (1 conditional delete); a re-added index starts from a fresh register, and a leftover one (a delete that failed) is overwritten by the create's CAS, so a stale `step_seq` or `running` status never blocks it. An action is idempotent as item 4 states per action (AC-7b), so a node that dies after its claim leaves a `running` record that the next evaluation after `serverless.ism.step_timeout` (default 10 min) re-claims at `step_seq + 1`; the dead node's late outcome CAS fails. Correctness is the compare-and-swap, never a lock or a leader. Only the top 2 nodes by placement try a claim, so a lost claim costs 1 PUT a step.

Planned later slice (gap, not built here): a document count derived from the write path, so `min_doc_count` and a rollover on a document count work without a poll (the owner's S3-cost principle rules a polled count out); until it lands those conditions are 400 at put and common rollover policies must use `min_index_age`.

**End state (owner's principle, 2026-11-28: no plugin relies on Java).** Every ISM route is native: `change_policy`, `retry`, `explain`, the policy and managed-index routes and the cluster settings all answer from the Rust store, no ISM route ends at a Java node, and Java is never in the runtime path or a fallback. Java may stay in tests for compatibility and for performance and memory comparison against the Rust build (the live-plugin run of `live_plugins.rs`, and shapes recorded from the stock plugin and compared offline, AC-13), beside tests that run on the Rust node alone. The ISM prefixes leave the plugin proxy's list in the change that completes the port, and the store and routes default on then (they cost nothing idle and, with the runner built, their policies run); the runner keeps its own switch, off until its standing registry reads are measured. Until then the routes stay off, so a deployment with a Java upstream never gets a half-native route set.

Not here: the Index Management plugin's rollup and transform jobs, snapshot management policies, the `_plugins/_ism/refresh_search_analyzers` route, the plugin's `.opendistro-ism-config` system index (a GET of it is 404), Java-shell interop (a Java node reading the Rust store).

## Acceptance criteria

- AC-1: a policy is created, read, replaced and deleted through the routes in the plugin's published shapes (`_id`, `_version`, `_seq_no`, `_primary_term`, `policy`); a stale `if_seq_no` is 409; a policy with an unknown state in a transition, an unsupported action or an unsupported condition is 400 `illegal_argument_exception` naming it; a missing policy is 404 `Policy not found`.
- AC-2: a policy create is 1 GET and 2 PUTs, a replace with `if_seq_no` adds the check's 2 GETs (the check and the put are a read then a put, not one swap: a replace racing another replace of the same policy can pass the check), a read 2 GETs, a delete 2 GETs and 1 PUT (the marker's swap, the templates' aggregate read; the content blob goes by a delete), none lists; a list reads the marker and one GET per object of its page of at most 100; each is a row in [request-budgets](../../request-budgets.md) that a test pins.
- AC-3: `add` attaches a policy and records the initial state; `explain` answers `index.plugins.index_state_management.policy_id`, `index`, `policy_id`, `policy_seq_no`, `enabled`, `state`, `action`, `step` and `info` in the plugin's shape; `remove` and `change_policy` act as the plugin's do; an index that does not exist is a failure entry, not a 500.
- AC-4: an index matching an `ism_template` is managed from creation and the highest priority wins; with ISM enabled a create costs exactly 1 more GET when no policy has a template and 2 more when some do and none matches, and with ISM off 0 more; two policies with the same pattern at the same priority are refused 400 at put; an attaching create adds the record's 1 GET and 2 PUTs, the register's create PUT and the registry's 1 GET and 1 PUT.
- AC-4b: `add` and `remove` cost the managed record's put (1 GET, 2 PUTs), the run register's create (1 PUT) or conditional delete, plus the registry's reads and writes of the protocol above (an adding `add`: 1 GET and 1 PUT of the shard; a removal leaving none: 1 GET of the shard, 1 GET of the marker, 1 PUT of the shard; a removal leaving others: 0 more); a tenant's 4,097th shard entry is 429; each is a row in the budgets.
- AC-5: with a mocked clock, an index in a state whose transition is `min_index_age: 7d` transitions on the first evaluation at or after the seventh day, and not before; a transition on `min_rollover_age` counts from the rollover time the record holds.
- AC-6: a `rollover` action rolls an alias or data stream once its condition holds, exactly once under two nodes racing the claim (asserted by the alias record's generation), and the managed record follows to the new index with the policy attached through `ism_template` or `rollover_alias`.
- AC-7: `delete`, `read_only`, `read_write` and `alias` actions have their effect on the index and its records, `explain` shows each step by the plugin's names, and the runner removes the managed record of an index that was deleted by someone else.
- AC-7b: each action re-run after an abandoned claim is a success with the same end state: a delete of a missing index, a snapshot that exists, a rollover whose alias already moved; an action past `action_timeout` is aborted and recorded failed.
- AC-8b: with 200 managed indices due in the same slot, every step completes, marker swaps retry at most 8 times each and never fail a step, and a marker left stale is corrected by the next `refresh_ttl` evaluation.
- AC-8: a step that fails retries as the policy's `retry` says by a derived evaluation time (a mocked clock moved past the backoff runs it, moved less does not), is `failed` past the count with the reason in `explain`, and `retry` re-arms it; a node that dies after claiming a step has it re-claimed after `step_timeout`, and the dead node's late outcome write fails.
- AC-9: **no poll per index**: with N managed indices, none due, the clock advanced one hour, the request meter reads exactly the registry reads (256 markers and the placed tenants' managed markers, each once per `refresh_ttl`), whatever N; the marker's `next_eval` is re-read only at that TTL, so an index is evaluated up to `refresh_ttl` late (300 s by default, the same bound alerting states) and never early; an index waiting on a derived time makes no request until that time, the creation date coming from the record. A cluster with no managed index reads the 256 markers per `refresh_ttl` and nothing more.
- AC-10: a step costs its claim PUT, the action's own requests and 1 PUT for the outcome on the run register, plus the record and marker update (1 GET, 2 PUTs) only when state, action, step, retry or `next_eval` changed; an evaluation that finds nothing due writes nothing.
- AC-11: memory is reserved before a policy or a record is held (4 x its bytes from the open-indexes pool); a tenant past 1,000 managed indices is 429 at add; a step whose work does not fit the pool is skipped and counted, not a 500.
- AC-12: with `OSS_ISM=false` every ISM route answers as today (the proxy when an upstream is set, else 400 `no handler found`), no registry is read and no step runs, and the existing proxy-path tests pass unchanged; the new tests run with it on (the default), and both run in CI.
- AC-14: the runner's decision is a pure function (`crates/node/src/ism_run/mod.rs`, unit tests): a record with no run yet is initialised (`Successfully initialized policy: <id>`) and asked again now; a state with no actions evaluates its transitions in order, the first met moving the record to its target state, and an unmet set stores `condition_not_met` with the earliest `min_index_age` or `min_rollover_age` moment as the next evaluation, so nothing polls; a rollover age with no recorded rollover, and a cron condition (not evaluated yet, a stated gap), have no derived time; a state with actions is left for the action runner.
- AC-13: the routes answer as the stock 3.10.0 recording does (`fixtures/stock-plugins/ism.json`, PR 996): `crates/node/tests/server/ism_stock.rs` replays its policy, template, explain, add, change_policy, retry and remove steps (all but the runner steps and the other plugins' list routes) against this node and compares each status and body, `<volatile>` and `<n>` as wildcards, and fails on any difference outside its `GAPS` list. The gaps, each stated here: the policy with every action (step 20) holds actions this node refuses at put (`force_merge`, `close`, `open`, `shrink`, `allocation`, `index_priority`, `notification`, size conditions), which also moves the template steps that follow it (21, 26, 27); a wildcard index name is refused 400 (steps 32, 43), since resolving one would list; the search errors of steps 11 and 43 lack `failed_shards` and `grouped`; the overlap answer (step 23) has no `caused_by`; and the index route's create answer also names `shards` (steps 25, 28, 29). The runner steps (57 to 66) are replayed once slice 3 exists.

## S3 request budget

Management: the stored-scripts shape, a row per route in [request-budgets](../../request-budgets.md) as each lands. Index create: +1 GET with ISM enabled (the policy marker, also when none exists), 0 with it off. Run: per step 1 claim PUT and 1 outcome PUT on the run register, the action's requests (a rollover, a delete and a block set are the existing routes' rows), and the record and marker update (1 GET, 2 PUTs) when something changed; an evaluation with nothing due writes nothing. Load: every node reads the 256 registry markers once per `refresh_ttl`, plus the managed marker of each tenant it places, so (256 + placed tenants) per TTL a node; it scales with nodes and tenants, never with indices, policies or time per index. LIST: none. Depth: one round for the claim, the action's own after it, one for the outcome.

## Switch

`serverless.ism.enabled` (`OSS_ISM`), default on, as for every plugin (Huy, 2026-11-28: no plugin needs a flag to be enabled); `OSS_ISM=false` is a kill switch that costs nothing, and with it off the ISM prefixes go to a plugin upstream when one is configured. The proxy's prefixes for ISM are removed when the port is complete. The runner has no switch of its own: its standing reads are bounded (256 registry markers and the placed tenants' markers every `refresh_ttl`, none for a tenant with no managed index) and measured as part of AC-8b. `refresh_ttl`, `step_timeout`, `action_timeout`, `max_managed_indices` (per tenant) and `max_policies` are limits with defaults, not switches. VERIFIED.md records, before the port is called complete: the stock recording of AC-13, the request counts of AC-2, AC-4, AC-4b, AC-9 and AC-10, a rollover and a delete run end to end on three nodes, and the memory of 1,000 managed indices held.

## Risks

- Reference behaviour is from the plugin's published documentation, not its source (`opensearch-project/index-management` is outside this sandbox's repositories); shapes are provisional until a stock recording exists.
- The registry shape is shared in intent with alerting; two copies drift if the alerting slice does not land first. The merge is a separate change.
- Refusing `min_doc_count` and size conditions loses a common rollover condition (`min_age` and `min_doc_count` together); a later write-path-derived count would restore it without a poll. The owner's call.
- A step that dies mid-action is re-run after `step_timeout`; an action that is not idempotent in some backend would run twice. The idempotence of each action is a test (AC-6, AC-7, AC-8), not an assumption.
- The policy-marker read on create adds a GET to every create while ISM is enabled; if the measurement shows it matters, the marker moves into the descriptor read the create already makes.
